# Measure first: extending the Arabic rehab coach

The way to make this PWA more advanced is not a bigger model. It is to turn what the app already sees into numbers a physiotherapist can trust, and to use LLMs, RAG and APIs only where the output is checked against data or approved by the physio. The evidence points one way. Phone-camera coaching is now standard across Hinge, Kaia/Sword, Medbridge, Kemtai, Exer and the UAE start-up Limb. Monocular joint angles still miss clinical accuracy by about 5-15 degrees. Timed functional tests (sit-to-stand, Timed Up and Go) reach ICCs above 0.9 even from a single device. Frontier vision-language models judge exercise form barely above chance. The project's own results say the same thing from the other side: a distilled Arabic BERT did not beat the 64 KB n-gram model, and Qwen2.5-7B labels did not pass the agreement gate. Labels and validation are the bottleneck, not model size. The defensible edge against well-funded competitors is a combination none of them documents: Egyptian-dialect Arabic, offline on-device inference with no video upload, a clinician-in-the-loop portal, and published validation numbers. That is also the software-engineering contribution the supervisor asked for: measurement pipelines, evaluation gates, data-minimising architecture and interoperability that a clinician could not build alone. The report ends with six ranked extensions, each with a pass rule. The first three are a validated "check-up" test mode, a Hinge-Robin-style flare-up flow built on a clinician-labelled evaluation loop, and a physio-side note drafter grounded in structured session data.

## Camera coaching is table stakes; the 2024-2026 race moved to scores, assistants and triage

Every leading digital MSK product now uses camera-only computer vision on a phone or tablet, so a MediaPipe-in-browser coach is mainstream rather than novel. Hinge Health's Movement Analysis (October 2025) measures joint angles, symmetry and endurance and combines them into "Hinge Scores". Its assistant Robin's first job is to recognise pain flare-ups, gather details and summarise them for the member's PT ([Hinge press release](https://www.hingehealth.com/resources/press-releases/hinge-health-unveils-new-ai-powered-care-tools/)). Sword Health's Phoenix (June 2024) is a voice agent that adjusts session difficulty in real time but "does not make any decisions independently" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/06/04/sword-health-announces-phoenix-conversational-ai.html)). Sword then bought Kaia for **$285M in January 2026**, merging the two leading camera-coaching apps ([Sword newsroom](https://swordhealth.com/newsroom/sword-acquires-kaia-health)). Medbridge added AI motion capture with fall-risk flags and a patient chatbot in May 2025 ([HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/2025/05/15/medbridge-expands-ai-motion-capture-technology-to-transform-msk-care/)). The US leaders price at **$799-1,000 per member per year**, half of it tied to outcomes ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250917895354/en/Alight-Expands-Partner-Network-With-the-Addition-of-Sword-Health); [Sword pricing](https://swordhealth.com/value/fair-pricing)). That price is impossible in Egypt and helps explain why the incumbents are absent.

Two patterns matter for the extensions. First, the industry's AI design is **"AI triages and summarises; the clinician decides"**. Hinge, Sword and Medbridge all say so publicly, and it matches this project's rule that the physio stays in charge. Second, vendor evidence is thin and self-funded. Hinge's S-1 discloses that all the outcome and ROI studies it discusses were sponsored by Hinge ([SEC S-1/A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1673743/000119312525121827/d829170ds1a.htm)). Exer, Kemtai, Physitrack and Limb publish no independent validation. The most credible benchmark a student can match is Kaia's 2021 agreement study, in which PTs agreed with the computer-vision corrections as often as with each other ([HealthTech HotSpot](https://healthtechhotspot.com/study-shows-computer-vision-technology-as-accurate-as-physical-therapists-in-suggesting-exercise-corrections/)). A published agreement statistic is therefore a real differentiator in its own right. The one MENA adherence datapoint is small but pointed: **71% of 80 surveyed UAE physio patients** did not do home exercises consistently ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/abu-dhabi/abu-dhabi-start-up-revolutionizes-physiotherapy-with-ai-powered-home-recovery-solution-1.500707309)). Limb's choice to collect footage of people in abayas and kandooras signals a measurable regional problem: public pose data is dominated by fitted sportswear. The same likely applies to galabeyas.

## Monocular angles miss clinical accuracy, but timed tests clear ICC 0.9

The 2026 scoping review of 117 markerless-pose studies finds sagittal lower-limb agreement with marker-based mocap clustering at **5-6 degrees**, "generally short of clinical acceptability". Frontal and transverse planes, older adults and patients are rarely validated ([arXiv 2609.18667](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18667)). MediaPipe specifically shows a hand/finger RMSE of **10.9 ± 7.8 degrees** ([Sensors 25(4):1079](https://doi.org/10.3390/s25041079)), lower-limb RMSE under 9 degrees and upper-limb around 15 degrees in a 2024 thesis ([Polimi](https://www.politesi.polimi.it/retrieve/285978a4-81d8-4962-99ce-4c93114044c8/2024_04_Francia_Tesi_01.pdf)), and a −17.5 degree hip bias in a VR-rehab validation ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/383968901_Validation_of_a_MediaPipe_System_for_Markerless_Motion_Analysis_During_Virtual_Reality_Rehabilitation)). The largest accuracy gains come from adding a biomechanical model and physics refinement on top of the keypoints, not from swapping the detector. OpenCap Monocular reaches **4.8 degrees rotational MAE** from one phone, against 9.3 degrees for a direct computer-vision baseline. That is a March 2026 preprint tested on 10 healthy adults ([arXiv 2603.24733](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24733)). It runs server-side and its WHAM/SMPL dependencies carry non-commercial licences. That makes it a candidate for an occasional physio-side "lab check", not the phone runtime. RTMPose-s in ONNX Runtime Web is the realistic stronger in-browser detector, at about 25 MB extra download and with no published Android FPS ([Harvard MMLA](https://mmla.gse.harvard.edu/tools/rtmpose/)). It would give up BlazePose's 3D world landmarks, so treat it as an A/B experiment rather than an assumed win.

Exercise-quality scoring shows the same ceiling. DeepRehabPile, the largest unified benchmark (60 tasks across KIMORE, UI-PRMD, KERAAL, IRDS, KINECAL, EHE, SPHERE and UCDHE), finds a small depthwise-separable CNN (LITEMV) best on average at **77.37% classification accuracy**. Its balanced accuracy is about 7 points lower, and STGCN graph networks sit near chance on binary correctness ([arXiv 2507.21018](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21018v1)). A 2026 preprint on REHAB24-6 reports interpretable MediaPipe angle templates at **0.58 balanced accuracy, and only 0.60 with a 16-camera optical system** ([Research Square](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-11822923/v1); numbers from snippet text, not peer-reviewed). If that holds, better pose barely helps; the labels and protocol design dominate. The project's own results fit this picture. The per-patient three-rep DTW reference reached macro-F1 0.892 on PhysioLens, but KERAAL AUC was 0.744 with a confidence interval of [0.489, 0.886], and trained models won within one dataset but lost across datasets (project results, 2026-11). A model that beats DTW in-distribution has not shown it generalises.

Vision-language models are not the answer for form judging. The CVPRW 2026 evaluation of Gemini 3.1 Pro, Qwen3-VL-235B and InternVL3.5 found best balanced accuracy of **0.603 on the Functional Movement Screen** and 0.56-0.57 on fitness sets, against a chance level near 0.5. Score correlations peaked around ρ = 0.28. The models tended to call every execution "correct" and shifted with superficial prompt wording ([arXiv 2604.08294](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08294v1)). Sending patient video to a cloud VLM would also be a cross-border transfer of biometric data. The rule that follows is to keep pose-rule and DTW checks as the judge and let language models only phrase what those checks measured.

Where cameras do reach clinical grade is **counting and timing**. The 2026 Ameya validation in 228 adults with chronic disease reports ICC(2,1) of **0.995 for five-times sit-to-stand, 0.962 for TUG, 0.928 for 30-second sit-to-stand and 0.868 for gait speed**, with 3.1% data loss ([PMC12773808](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12773808/)). The caveats are real: it used a LiDAR iPad on a tripod in clinic, tracking worsened with dark or loose clothing, and gait speed relied on depth. Timing tests depend on event detection (seat-off, stand, turn), not precise angles, so they transfer to RGB BlazePose far better than ROM does. No RGB-only BlazePose validation with ICCs was found, which leaves a clear gap for this project to fill. Longitudinal progress has a similar issue: no study validates camera-measured ROM change against a minimum detectable change. The app should estimate its own test-retest noise and flag "progress" only above it ([Sensors 25(19):6239](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/19/6239) cites 50-70% home-programme non-adherence, so adherence counts are a sturdier portal metric than absolute angles).

## Language models earn their place on the physio side, behind data and approval

The best-evidenced LLM use near rehab is drafting documentation from data, not prescribing. A study of 12,999 clinician-annotated sentences measured **1.47% hallucination and 3.45% omission** in generated clinical notes, and both fell with iterative pipeline fixes ([medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.12.24313556.full.pdf)). On SOAP-format therapy notes, therapists preferred LLM drafts in blind tests even though the drafts contained hallucinations, and LLM-as-judge struggled to catch those hallucinations ([TN-Eval, ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-industry.14.pdf)). So the check has to be structural or human, not another LLM. Time savings are modest: in a 238-physician RCT only one of two ambient scribes reduced time-in-note ([PMC12265753](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12265753/)). The "40-60% reduction" figures in secondary sources are marketing-grade. Autonomous exercise prescription is not supported. LLM-generated MSK programmes score only moderate on DISCERN (human mean 50.48), safety and contraindications were never quantified, and readability (FKGL 7.3, SMOG 10.1) sits above patient level ([PMC13465567](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13465567/)). When therapists prescribe and the LLM only transcribes, delivery was correct in 397 of 398 cases, but 2.5% of instructions still contained hallucinated monitoring logic ([arXiv 2511.18274](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18274)).

RAG reduces fabrication without removing it. A German orthopaedic patient-education RAG reached **faithfulness about 0.85** on RAGAS, and its authors say retrieval "cannot eliminate" made-up answers ([PMC12551339](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12551339/)). Roughly one answer in seven carrying unsupported content is unacceptable for unsupervised patient advice. The safe pattern for this project is therefore **extractive** RAG: retrieve and show a physio-approved snippet verbatim, and refuse when nothing matches. The LLM's role moves to authoring time, drafting Egyptian-Arabic education text that the physio approves once and the app serves offline. Corpus licensing narrows the options. WHO's Package of Interventions for Rehabilitation is CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO, which is usable for a non-commercial thesis with share-alike derivatives ([WHO IRIS](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/e2101cd7-a3ef-4c87-a482-a9f1960179ca/content)). NICE forbids AI use without a signed licence ([NICE](https://www.nice.org.uk/reusing-our-content/use-of-our-content-for-ai-purposes)), and Physiopedia's terms forbid scraping and data mining for AI ([Physiopedia](https://physio-pedia.com/Physiopedia:AI_Training_Model_Licensing_Policy_An_Open_Letter)).

For Arabic NLP, general frontier models beat dedicated Arabic medical LLMs on independent benchmarks. Gemini 2.5 Pro scored 74% on a blind shared-task test while BiMediX2 "lagged general models" ([ArabicNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.arabicnlp-sharedtasks.20.pdf); [MedArabiQ](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v298/daoud25a.html)). These benchmarks are MSA exam and QA style, and **no Egyptian-dialect triage or red-flag benchmark exists**. That fits the project's own findings. Qwen2.5-7B's kappa of 0.52 against the 0.60 gate and the BERT distillation's 90/105 vs 92/105 both point to the labels rather than the model (project results, 2026-11). A larger frontier model may label better, but only an offline, development-time pass on public or consented text can show it, measured against the doctor's 60 labels through the same gate.

Speech is a narrow opportunity. Zero-shot Whisper-large-v3 scores about **48-50% WER on Egyptian Arabic**, and GPT-4o-transcribe about 31% ([arXiv 2609.35564](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35564v1)). A fine-tuned Whisper-small self-reports about 22.7% ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/MAdel121/whisper-small-egyptian-arabic)). Free-form patient speech is therefore unreliable, but a closed command vocabulary ("stop", "pain", a 0-10 score) on whisper-tiny/base via transformers.js and WebGPU is feasible offline ([HF transformers.js](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers.js/en/guides/webgpu)). Azure lists two Egyptian neural TTS voices, ar-EG-SalmaNeural and ar-EG-ShakirNeural ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/language-support)). Rendering a fixed cue library once at build time gives zero-latency, offline, non-hallucinating voice coaching. A live cloud voice loop does not: one posture-feedback prototype had to abandon Gemini when rate limits halted its feedback ([ICDVRAT](https://journals.aau.dk/index.php/ICDVRAT/article/download/11716/9100)). Reminder APIs (WhatsApp, SMS, Web Push) are cheap, but the best RCT found that behaviour-change SMS raised self-reported adherence **without improving clinical outcomes** ([PMC7551110](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7551110/)). They are worth building, but only with claims about adherence, not outcomes.

Regulation turns the offline design into an asset. Egypt's PDPL treats health and biometric data as sensitive ([law text](https://www.acc.com/sites/default/files/program-materials/upload/Data%20Protection%20Law%20-%20Egypt%20-%20EN%20-%20MBH.PDF)). Its executive regulations (Decree 816/2025) are now in force, with compliance due around 31 October / 1 November 2026 ([Shalakany](https://shalakany.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/New-Executive-Regulations-for-Egypts-Personal-Data-Protection-Law-.pdf); [e-cens](https://e-cens.com/blog/egypt-data-protection-law-compliance-guide/)). Cross-border transfer needs equivalent protection and a PDPC licence ([recordinglaw.com](https://www.recordinglaw.com/world-laws/world-data-privacy-laws/egypt-data-privacy-laws/)). Any cloud LLM or ASR call carrying patient text or voice is exactly that kind of transfer. WHO's 2024 guidance on large multimodal models warns specifically about automation bias and calls for post-release audits ([WHO IRIS](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/e9e62c65-6045-481e-bd04-20e206bc5039/content)). Logging every LLM draft and the physio's edits satisfies that and produces the measurement data at the same time. For interoperability, the HL7 Physical Activity IG already models exercise prescription with CarePlan, Observation, Goal and QuestionnaireResponse ([HL7](https://hl7.org/fhir/us/physical-activity/)). Egypt's Universal Health Insurance system is rolling out unified EMRs but exposes no public FHIR API ([Ahram Online](https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/573309.aspx)). FHIR-shaped storage and export is therefore preparation for later integration, not an integration available today.

## The data that moves the needle is CC0 test data and clinician labels, not more gym images

Of the public data found, three sets would change what the project can claim. **KINECAL** (PhysioNet, CC0) has 90 participants aged 18-92 recorded with Kinect skeletons doing TUG, five-times sit-to-stand, a 3 m walk and eight balance stances, labelled with SPPB scores and published at-risk thresholds: STS-5 over 16.7 s, TUG over 14 s ([PhysioNet](https://physionet.org/content/kinecal/)). It is 86 GB zipped, so download only the TUG and STS folders, and the Kinect joints need projecting to MediaPipe-like 2D. **Rehab-Pile** packages the field's benchmarks with common splits and code ([arXiv 2507.21018](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21018)). Results from the project's quality model become directly comparable to the literature, and UCDHE adds a 2D OpenPose-based error-type set close to the phone setting. Licences have to be checked per source. A **CC BY 4.0 Mendeley set** (July 2026) of smartphone gym videos from 26 people, seven exercises and three views, labelled good or bad form, is the only commercially clean phone-camera form set found ([Mendeley](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/kgbb3yn47p/2)). It gives a direct test of how robust the checks are to camera angle.

For language, **AHD/AHQAD** offers 808k Arabic patient Q&A pairs in 90 categories from Altibbi ([Mendeley](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/mgj29ndgrk/2)). It is a natural pool from which to draw new blind titles for the pain check and to test retrieval, but its licence is unverified and overlap with Shifaa must be removed first. **Common Voice Arabic 23.0** (CC0, 158 h) is the only legally clean speech set found, with its Egyptian share unknown ([Mozilla Data Collective](https://datacollective.mozillafoundation.org/datasets/cmflnuzw47jng4x81tavetpul)). Egyptian corpora such as Masri 100h and EgArbCSC are non-commercial ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ehabnegm/100-hour-Egyptian-dataset-single-speaker); [MagicHub](https://magichub.com/datasets/egyptian-arabic-conversational-speech-corpus/)).

Roboflow and Kaggle add less than their download counts suggest. The Roboflow sets are small, community-labelled gym keypoint sets of hundreds of images with no clinician labels ([Roboflow search](https://universe.roboflow.com/search?p=2&q=class%3Asquat)). They only matter if the app moves to a fine-tunable pose model. Kaggle exercise sets mostly carry exercise-class labels under non-commercial licences ([Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/hasyimabdillah/workoutfitness-video)). The coordinator's own browser checks found a few items worth a manual look, with licences and labels unverified. One is a 749-image Roboflow hand-physiotherapy set (finger abduction/adduction, extension, fist, MCP flexion, opposition). Hand rehab would be a new body region, and MediaPipe Hands' ~11 degree finger RMSE suggests it could count reps and positions well but not grade angles. The others are the Kaggle PHYSIO-DATASET of real-world home physiotherapy video, MEx (seven physiotherapy exercises, multimodal), DMD-BAK (2,691 back photos from 139 volunteers doing 19 exercises), and wrist IMU data, which is not camera data. **No public Arabic or Middle-East rehab motion dataset exists**, and no public ROM video set with goniometer ground truth was found. A small self-collected Egyptian set (phone video reduced to keypoints, physio scores, loose clothing, Arabic pain notes) would therefore be novel and the strongest thesis contribution. It would also serve as test data for every extension below.

### 1. A validated check-up mode turns the camera into a clinical instrument

The physio prescribes a guided test: 30-second sit-to-stand count, five-times sit-to-stand time, or TUG over a marked 3 m line. The app detects seat-off, full stand and turn events from BlazePose hip and knee trajectories, times or counts them on-device, and charts the result against KINECAL's published at-risk thresholds in the portal. Results are exported as FHIR Observations under the Physical Activity IG. Sessions are flagged as "changed" only when the change exceeds the app's own test-retest minimum detectable change. This is the highest-value extension because timing and counting are where camera measurement is already validated (ICC 0.93-0.995 in Ameya). No competitor or paper has published an RGB-only, low-end-Android version, and the work is mostly software engineering: event detection, calibration prompts, a replay harness and agreement statistics. **Pass rule:** on at least 20 adults recorded in Egypt, including some in loose clothing, ICC(2,1) is ≥ 0.90 for 5xSTS time and 30s-STS count and ≥ 0.85 for TUG against a physio's stopwatch. Bland-Altman limits are reported. The KINECAL replay must classify the at-risk threshold with kappa ≥ 0.80 against the dataset labels. **Risks:** the projected Kinect skeletons test the timing logic, not BlazePose itself. Tracking degrades with loose or dark clothing (Ameya's own limitation). Gait speed without depth needs floor markers, so it should stay out of scope until it is validated separately.

### 2. A flare-up flow built on clinician-gated labels extends the existing differentiator

The existing 64 KB on-device pain check becomes the trigger for a structured, offline follow-up: pain location, NPRS 0-10 and a fixed set of red-flag questions written by the physio. The result goes into a portal review queue, with "stop and contact your physio / seek urgent care" wording on the patient side. In parallel, the engineering goes into the labelling pipeline rather than the model. AHD supplies fresh, de-duplicated Arabic titles. A frontier LLM, run at development time on public text only, acts as a second labeller. Its labels enter training only if they pass the existing kappa ≥ 0.60 gate against the doctor's 60 labels, and disagreements go to the doctor for adjudication. This mirrors Robin's first task exactly, but keeps patient text on the phone, which neither Hinge nor Sword documents, and it attacks the bottleneck the project's own experiments identified. **Pass rule:** any new pain model ships only if it catches at least 92/105 on the frozen blind set and does not lower red-flag sensitivity on the doctor-labelled titles (reported with Wilson intervals). An automated network test shows that zero patient text leaves the device without a consent flag, and 100% of flags reach the portal queue after sync. **Risks:** 60 doctor labels give wide confidence intervals, so the report must say "non-inferior within the uncertainty", not "better". AHD's licence and overlap with Shifaa must be checked. A patient-facing triage feature sits closer to medical-device territory, so it stays a prompt to contact the physio, never a diagnosis.

### 3. A grounded note drafter is the best-evidenced LLM feature

In the portal, the physio clicks "draft note" for a patient's week. The app assembles de-identified structured JSON: reps, completion, DTW template-match fraction, check-up results, NPRS trend and flags. An LLM writes a SOAP-style progress note and a short Egyptian-Arabic summary for the patient. Every sentence must cite the data field it rests on, and a deterministic checker rejects any sentence without a valid citation or with a number that does not match the data. The physio edits and signs, and the system logs the edit distance and time to sign. This is where hallucination rates are lowest in the literature (1.47% per sentence), and the structural grounding check is something a software engineer can build and a clinician cannot. Its value is a physio-time claim, which is exactly how Hinge positions Robin's summaries. **Pass rule:** 100% of shipped sentences pass the field-citation checker. On a human audit of at least 30 drafts, unsupported sentences are ≤ 2% and omissions of flagged events are ≤ 5%. Median time to sign is lower than writing manually, paired over the same patients. **Risks:** LLM-as-judge cannot be trusted to detect hallucinations (TN-Eval), so the audit must be human. A cloud call is a PDPL cross-border transfer even with structured data, so send only de-identified fields after consent, or use a locally hosted model. Reasoning modes can harm SOAP quality ([arXiv 2605.24902](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24902v2)), so keep the prompt plain.

### 4. Offline extractive RAG answers questions without generating text at runtime

Patients ask in Egyptian Arabic ("can I do this if my knee is swollen?"). A small on-device index (BM25 over character n-grams, which matches the project's proven text features, or a compact multilingual embedding model) retrieves from a corpus of physio-approved answers. The physio drafts the corpus with an LLM at authoring time from WHO PIR and their own leaflets, rewritten to at most grade-6 readability. The app shows the approved snippet verbatim with its source. Anything below a retrieval threshold, or anything that trips the pain check, is refused and routed to the flare-up flow. Faithfulness is 1.0 by construction because nothing is generated at runtime, which turns RAG's known ~0.85 faithfulness weakness into a design argument. **Pass rule:** on 100 physio-written dialect paraphrases of in-scope questions, top-1 retrieval is ≥ 85%. On 50 out-of-scope or red-flag questions, refusal or routing is ≥ 95%. Every served answer carries a physio approval record. **Risks:** dialect paraphrase retrieval can underperform, writing and approving content takes physio time, and WHO PIR's NC-SA licence restricts any future commercial use.

### 5. Pre-rendered Egyptian voice cues give coaching without the cloud

A fixed library of a few hundred Egyptian-Arabic coaching phrases ("انزل شوية", "باقي ٣ عدات") is rendered once with Azure ar-EG voices, or recorded by a native speaker, and cached by the service worker. The existing pose rules pick which clip plays. An optional closed-vocabulary voice-command layer runs whisper-tiny/base through transformers.js, with a VAD gate, for "stop", "pain" and 0-10 scores, and feeds spoken pain words into the existing n-gram check. Sword and Limb treat spoken feedback as core, and Egyptian dialect plus offline operation is unclaimed. **Pass rule:** on recordings from at least 10 Egyptian speakers in home-like noise, command accuracy is ≥ 95% and false triggers are ≤ 1 per 10-minute session. The physio signs off every cue. **Risks:** dialect ASR is weak (48-50% WER zero-shot for large models, worse for tiny), so free speech must stay out of scope. Whisper-base adds about 200 MB to the offline cache, which is heavy on low-end phones. The Azure terms need checking for build-time rendering.

### 6. A benchmarked error-type model is a research stretch, not a product bet

A LITEMV-style depthwise-separable 1D-CNN runs on DTW-aligned angle sequences and outputs per-joint, per-phase error types rather than a single correct/incorrect label. It is trained and reported on Rehab-Pile's standard splits with balanced accuracy, and tested for camera-view robustness on the Mendeley smartphone set. Its value is academic comparability: a leaderboard row against published baselines. **Pass rule:** it ships only if it beats the DTW reference's macro-F1 0.892 on PhysioLens and also beats DTW in leave-one-dataset-out evaluation including KERAAL. Otherwise the result is published as a documented negative finding, as the BERT distillation was. **Risks:** the project has already seen trained models lose across datasets, and REHAB24-6 suggests a ceiling near 0.6 balanced accuracy set by the labels themselves. Rehab-Pile licences are inherited per source.

## Conclusion

The project's two negative results (the BERT distillation and the Qwen gate) are not setbacks. They are the template for the product. The market leaders charge $800-1,000 per member and publish sponsored outcomes. A graduation project cannot match their scale, but it can do something they mostly do not: put a frozen test set, a pre-declared pass rule and an agreement statistic behind every feature, and refuse to ship what fails. Seen this way, the advanced version of the coach is an evaluation-gated system in which LLMs draft, retrieval quotes approved text, and the physio signs.

The ranking also says what to leave out. VLM form judging, autonomous exercise prescription, live cloud voice agents and federated learning all look advanced but fail on measured accuracy, hallucination, offline use or PDPL. The two assets that cannot be downloaded are a doctor-labelled Egyptian-Arabic pain set and a small Egyptian phone-video check-up set with stopwatch ground truth. Both will outlast any model choice and would be the first public data of their kind for the region. Several facts behind this report remain unverified: the Kaggle and Roboflow licences and labels, the AHD licence, adult Arabic KOOS/PSFS validation, and any SaMD guidance from the Egyptian Drug Authority. Each needs checking before the related extension is presented as finished.
